What have we done so far?

So far we have built the structure of the page and the first main figure, a dumbbell chart of the life expectancy gap between women and men. We used World Bank data on life expectancy at birth by sex. We took out regional and income-group totals so that each row is a real country, and we worked out the gap (female minus male, in years) for every country and year from 1960 to 2019. Figure 1 shows the 30 most populous countries, each with an open dot for its 1960 gap and a filled dot for its 2019 gap. The line between the dots is pink if the gap widens, blue if it narrows, and gray if it changes by less than half a year. Making the graph interactive allows exploring life expectancy across the 30 countries by different start and end years, sorting based on different factors of interest, and diving in deeper to a specific country of interest by exploring their life expectancy change over time.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The hardest part will be to make sure the interactive graphs in our final version teach the reader something a static chart can’t. A year slider seems simple, but dots that jump around are hard to follow, so the change needs smooth, consistent transitions to read as one story. A further challenge is explaining why the gap moves, through factors such as maternal health, smoking, war, and the alcohol-related deaths behind Russia’s 10-year gap. That means linking this chart to other World Bank indicators without crowding the page. Finally, the story needs a clear hook, middle and take away so the reader is guided instead of just shown data.
