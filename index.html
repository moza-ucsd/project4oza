Overview

Women outlive men in nearly every country on Earth, but how much longer they live, and what that means for societies, is far less obvious. This article uses World Bank data from 1960 to 2019 to ask a simple question: who outlives whom? We start with the 30 most populous countries and watch the gap between women's and men's life expectancy grow, shrink and, in a few places, flip. Then we widen the lens to every country with data: does the gap track how long people live overall, who actually survives to age 65, and how are longer lives changing the share of older people in a population? Every figure is interactive, so drag the sliders, press Play and click around to explore the story yourself.

Point 1

Women now outlive men in the world's biggest countries

In 2019, women outlived men in all 30 of the world's most populous countries. That was not always true: in 1960 men lived longer in Bangladesh, India and Iran, while the gap was already around 7 to 8 years in Russia, South Korea and Vietnam. Since then the gap has widened by more than half a year in 18 of these countries, narrowed in 6 and barely moved in 6. Use the story buttons, drag the years or press Play to see which countries moved the most.
